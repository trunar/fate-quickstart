## Usage

Hooks are grouped by **scale** – how much of the world the problem touches and how long it's likely to run. Grab one, rename the proper nouns, and drop it in front of your players.

Each entry has:

- **The Situation** – what is already true before the players arrive
- **The Hook** – what pulls the players in
- **Inaction** – what happens if nobody does anything

Global aspects are not included here – they're a table-level decision. Once the players engage and the situation becomes concrete, you may want to place one or two situation aspects on the world to reflect the mood or stakes.

---

## Single Session

Short, contained, resolves in one sitting. One problem, one location, one clock.

---

### The Wrong Body

**The Situation** – A body has turned up somewhere it shouldn't be. The wrong part of town, the wrong building, the wrong bed. Someone powerful is connected to it and wants it handled quietly.

**The Hook** – The players are either the ones being hired to make it go away, the ones who found it, or the ones being blamed for it.

**Inaction** – The wrong person takes the fall. The powerful party covers their tracks permanently, including removing anyone who got too close.

---

### The Last Train Out

**The Situation** – Something is coming. A storm, an army, a creature, a catastrophe. Everyone who can leave already has. The players are still here, and so is someone who can't make it out alone.

**The Hook** – Get the person out before whatever is coming arrives. The clock is literal.

**Inaction** – Whatever is coming arrives. The person left behind doesn't survive it. The players might.

---

### The Auction

**The Situation** – Something significant is being sold tonight. An artifact, a person, information, a weapon. Multiple interested parties are in the room, none of them friendly to each other or to the players.

**The Hook** – The players need to acquire the thing, prevent the sale, or find out who the buyer is before the hammer falls.

**Inaction** – The item changes hands. Whoever bought it uses it, and the players find out what it was worth when the consequences arrive later.

---

### The Locked Room

**The Situation** – A group of people are stuck somewhere together. A building, a vessel, a bunker. One of them has done something terrible. The exits won't open until someone figures out who.

**The Hook** – The players are among the trapped, or have been brought in to resolve it. Everyone has a reason to lie.

**Inaction** – The group fractures. The guilty party escapes in the chaos or eliminates the remaining witnesses.

---

### The Favor

**The Situation** – Someone the players owe, trust, or care about asks for help with something small. It isn't small.

**The Hook** – The task escalates immediately upon arrival. What looked like an errand is a crisis. The person who asked may not have known, or may have.

**Inaction** – The person who asked pays the price the players avoided. The debt between them changes shape.

---

### The Negotiation

**The Situation** – Two parties are about to do something destructive to each other and everyone nearby. A deal is theoretically possible. Nobody trusts anyone enough to make it.

**The Hook** – The players are brought in as neutral parties, leverage, translators, or witnesses. They may not actually be neutral.

**Inaction** – The violence happens. The collateral damage includes someone or something the players care about.

---

### The Replacement

**The Situation** – Someone needs to be somewhere they can't be. A meeting, a performance, a formal occasion. The players are being asked to provide a substitute, play the role themselves, or cover for an absence that can't be explained.

**The Hook** – The occasion turns out to matter more than advertised, and the absence has a reason nobody mentioned.

**Inaction** – The absence is noticed by the wrong people. What it signals to them sets something larger in motion.

---

### The Evidence

**The Situation** – Something incriminating exists and is about to be found, used, or destroyed. It might implicate a player character. It might implicate someone they're protecting. It might prove something true that powerful people want kept false.

**The Hook** – Get to it first. Or decide whether to.

**Inaction** – It surfaces in the worst possible context. The people it implicates face consequences they can't escape, and the players' connection to it becomes known.

---

## Multi-Session Arc

A story with a beginning, middle, and end. Three to six sessions. One central problem that gets worse before it gets better.

---

### The Succession

**The Situation** – A position of power is vacant or about to be. A throne, a seat, a criminal empire, a corporation's top floor. Multiple factions are maneuvering. The legitimate heir, if there is one, is not the obvious winner.

**The Hook** – The players are entangled – as operatives of one faction, as the heir's protectors, as people who knew the previous holder, or as wildcards everyone is trying to use.

**Inaction** – The wrong faction wins. Their consolidation of power closes doors, removes protections, and makes the players' lives structurally worse. The window to affect the outcome closes permanently.

---

### The Disappeared

**The Situation** – People have been vanishing. Not randomly – there's a pattern, but it isn't obvious yet. The official explanation is wrong. The people looking for answers keep running into the same walls.

**The Hook** – Someone close to the players is among the missing, or the players are hired to find one of them, or they stumble into the pattern while doing something else entirely.

**Inaction** – The disappearances continue and accelerate. By the time the pattern becomes undeniable, the scale of what's happened is too large to quietly fix.

---

### The Debt Comes Due

**The Situation** – Something from the past is returning. A promise made, a wrong done, a deal struck, an enemy who wasn't finished. The players may not all know each other's history with it.

**The Hook** – The past arrives in the present with specific demands. The players must decide how much they're willing to pay to close it.

**Inaction** – The debt is collected anyway, on the creditor's terms. Collateral damage extends to everyone the players have built relationships with since then.

---

### The Contamination

**The Situation** – Something is spreading. A disease, an idea, a substance, a corrupting influence. It started small and contained. It isn't anymore. The people responsible either can't stop it or won't.

**The Hook** – The players are affected, adjacent, or in a position to do something about it. Everyone else with that position has already been neutralized.

**Inaction** – The spread reaches a threshold that makes containment impossible. Whatever it is becomes the new normal, and the world is measurably worse.

---

### The Trial

**The Situation** – Someone is being held accountable for something – a crime, a failure, a truth. The proceeding is formal but not fair. The outcome has been decided by people who aren't in the room.

**The Hook** – The players are defending, investigating, or trying to expose the machinery behind the verdict. Or one of them is the one on trial.

**Inaction** – The verdict stands. The person condemned disappears into whatever system holds them. The precedent set makes the next injustice easier.

---

### The Heist

**The Situation** – Something needs to be taken, moved, destroyed, or exposed. It is very well protected. Multiple parties want it. The window for action is narrow and getting narrower.

**The Hook** – The players are recruited, hired, or desperate enough to attempt it. The planning is as important as the execution.

**Inaction** – Someone else gets there first, or the window closes. What they do with it is worse than what the players would have done.

---

### The Reckoning

**The Situation** – A wrong was done, years ago, by people who have since built comfortable lives on top of it. The evidence still exists. So do the victims, or what's left of them.

**The Hook** – The players are the ones who find out, or are hired by someone who already knows, or are connected to either side of the original wrong.

**Inaction** – The people responsible continue undisturbed. The victims get nothing. One of them eventually takes matters into their own hands in a way that costs everyone more than justice would have.

---

### The Siege

**The Situation** – A place the players care about – a community, a building, a territory, a person – is under sustained pressure. Not a single attack but a slow, deliberate campaign to take it, break it, or drive everyone out.

**The Hook** – The players are inside the siege, or return to find it underway, or are the reason the pressure started.

**Inaction** – The place falls. What replaces it is worse, and the people who were there are scattered or gone.

---

## Campaign Scale

Slow burns. Problems that reshape the world if unaddressed. These take a full campaign to resolve and should be introduced early, developed through play, and allowed to change shape as the players act.

---

### The Succession of Empires

**The Situation** – A dominant power is collapsing. Not quickly – slowly, structurally, in ways that most people haven't accepted yet. The factions positioning to fill the vacuum are all flawed. The collapse will reshape everything.

**The Hook** – The players are caught in the transition. They may be agents of one faction, survivors of the collapse, or people trying to build something new in the wreckage.

**Inaction** – The vacuum fills itself, badly. The faction that wins is the one least constrained by conscience. The world that emerges is stable in the way a boot on a throat is stable.

---

### The Slow Catastrophe

**The Situation** – Something is wrong at a systemic level. The environment, the economy, the magical order, the social fabric. It has been wrong for a long time. The people who benefit from it have suppressed the evidence. The tipping point is approaching.

**The Hook** – The players encounter the human scale of the problem – specific people, specific places, specific costs – before they understand what they're actually looking at.

**Inaction** – The tipping point is reached. What happens after is irreversible. The people who caused it are insulated from consequences and the players aren't.

---

### The Old Enemy Returns

**The Situation** – Something that was defeated, buried, sealed, or forgotten is coming back. It may not be evil – it may simply be what it always was, and the world has changed around it. Or it may be exactly as bad as the stories say, and the stories were understated.

**The Hook** – Signs appear. People who knew what to look for are dying or disappearing. The players are among the first to understand what the signs mean.

**Inaction** – The return completes itself without opposition. Whatever this thing is, it now exists in the world again, and the generation that knew how to fight it is gone.

---

### The Great Work

**The Situation** – Someone, or a group, is building something. A weapon, a nation, an ideology, an artifact, a god. They have been at it for a long time and they are close. The work is almost certainly going to cause tremendous harm, but it is not entirely without merit, and not everyone involved is a villain.

**The Hook** – The players become aware of the work in progress. They may be recruited to assist, tasked to stop it, or simply in the way.

**Inaction** – The work is completed. Its effects are permanent and world-altering. Whether those effects are purely terrible or terrible in complicated ways depends on what the players did along the way.

---

### The Hidden War

**The Situation** – A conflict has been ongoing for a long time, beneath the surface of ordinary life. Most people don't know it exists. The players stumble into it and cannot un-stumble.

**The Hook** – They witness something they shouldn't, are mistaken for operatives of one side, or one of them is revealed to have always been connected to it.

**Inaction** – The hidden war continues. One side gains enough advantage to surface it – bringing the conflict into the open in a way that forces everyone to pick a side whether they want to or not.

---

### The Prophecy Problem

**The Situation** – A prophecy exists, is widely believed, and is shaping events whether or not it's true. Factions are maneuvering to fulfill or prevent it. Someone has decided one of the players is the subject of it. They may be right.

**The Hook** – The players are named, implicated, or simply inconveniently present at a prophesied moment. Now everyone with an interest in the prophecy has an interest in them.

**Inaction** – The prophecy fulfills itself through the actions of those who believed it, regardless of whether it was ever actually true. What it promised arrives, and it is not what anyone wanted.

---

### The Inheritance

**The Situation** – Something vast – a power, a responsibility, a curse, a kingdom, a truth – is being passed down, and the players are the ones it's being passed to. They did not ask for it. The previous holder may not have survived it.

**The Hook** – The inheritance arrives before the players are ready, understand what it is, or have agreed to accept it. It comes with enemies already in motion.

**Inaction** – The inheritance goes unclaimed or falls to someone who will use it badly. What it represented – protection, potential, balance – is lost with it.

---

## A Note on Impending Doom

Every hook above has a consequence of inaction. These aren't punishments – they're the world being a world. Players should feel that their choices matter because the alternative is real, not because the GM is withholding the story until they engage correctly.

The best impending doom is specific, visible, and personal. An army marching on a city is abstract. The family that runs the bakery the players ate breakfast at, who will not survive the siege, is not.

If you want to set a global aspect to frame a hook once it's in play, keep it to one or two, make them observable rather than explanatory, and let the players help name them when they understand what they're facing.
