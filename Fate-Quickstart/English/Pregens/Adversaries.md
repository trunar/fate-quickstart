## Usage

Adversaries are organized by **tier** and **role**. Grab any entry, rename it to fit your setting, and use as-is or tweak one aspect. Skill values assume Fate Core; for Fate Accelerated, substitute the closest Approach.

Stress and consequences follow standard Fate Core defaults unless noted.

---

## Nameless NPCs

Nameless NPCs have no consequence slots. They absorb stress until they're taken out. Use them in groups. When a nameless NPC hits zero stress, they're out – no negotiation.

> **Mobs:** Treat a group of 2–4 nameless NPCs as a single unit with +1 skill per additional member (max +4 total). The mob is taken out when it runs out of stress, not individuals.

_A mirror, a foil, or a former ally. Built like a PC. Memorable._

**Aspects**

## Boss-Level Notes

Bosses are intentionally not pre-statted here. A boss that works for one campaign is background noise in another.

When building a boss, consider:

**Structure**

- 3–5 aspects including a High Concept that encodes their power and a Trouble that is a genuine vulnerability
- Skills at +4 and +5 in their core areas
- Full consequence track: Mild / Moderate / Severe, plus one Extreme if they're the final antagonist
- 2–3 stunts, at least one of which changes the nature of the conflict rather than just adding a bonus

**Design Principles**

- Give them something the players can't just fight through – a social shield, a hostage, a position of power, a moral complication
- Their Trouble should be something the players can discover and exploit
- At least one stunt should create a moment, not just a modifier
- Consider a _Countdown Aspect_ – a situation aspect that changes the scene if the players don't address it

**Suggested Consequence Add-On** When a boss takes their Moderate consequence, something changes – reinforcements arrive, they reveal a new capability, the environment shifts, or their demeanor cracks. This makes the fight feel like it has phases without requiring a separate mechanic.
